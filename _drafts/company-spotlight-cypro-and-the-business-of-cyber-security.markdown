This week we're having a look at [CyPro](https://cypro.co.uk/about/), the Canary Wharf based cyber security company helping growing businesses expand securely; and our partner for company visits. 

#### A Different Kind of Cyber Security Company

CyPro works primarily with growing companies that need serious cyber security expertise but may not yet require, or be able to build, a large internal security department.

The business was founded in 2021 by Rob McBride and Jonny Pelter. Both had worked in major professional services firms, including Deloitte and KPMG, before advising governments, banks and businesses on cyber security.

Their idea grew from a frustration with the traditional consultancy model. Senior experts often helped to win the work, only for much of the delivery to be passed to junior teams. CyPro set out to offer growing businesses more direct access to experienced specialists, at a cost suited to their size.

The company has since grown into an employee owned business with more than 40 specialists. Its team includes security engineers, risk and resilience consultants, virtual Chief Information Security Officers, data privacy experts and Security Operations Centre analysts.

#### A Fast Growing Industry

The [UK cyber security sector generated £14.7 billion in annual revenue in 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cyber-security-sectoral-analysis-2026), an increase of 11 per cent on the previous year. The country now has more than 2,600 cyber security firms employing almost 70,000 people.

Demand is growing for an uncomfortable reason. The Government’s latest [Cyber Security Breaches Survey](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/cyber-security-breaches-survey-20252026/cyber-security-breaches-survey-20252026) found that 43 per cent of British businesses had identified a cyber breach or attack during the previous twelve months. That represents an estimated 612,000 companies.

For a growing business, an attack can mean more than a technical interruption. It can stop trading, expose customer information, damage trust and delay investment. Weak security can also prevent a company from winning contracts with larger organisations that expect their suppliers to meet strict standards.

#### What Does CyPro Actually Do?

[CyPro’s services](https://cypro.co.uk/services/) are divided into two broad areas: building protection and maintaining it.

The first begins with understanding the business. What information does it hold? Which systems matter most? Where are the weaknesses, and what would happen if an important service suddenly failed?

CyPro can then create a security strategy, assess risks, prepare incident and disaster recovery plans, train staff and help the organisation work towards standards such as ISO 27001, Cyber Essentials Plus and SOC 2.

The second area is continuous support. CyPro provides virtual security leadership, vulnerability testing, penetration testing and round the clock monitoring. Its specialists can investigate suspicious activity, respond to incidents and examine how attackers might try to enter an organisation.

This model allows a smaller company to draw on a complete security team without recruiting every specialist itself. The level of support can then expand as the business grows.

#### The New Challenge of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence is changing both sides of cyber security. Criminals can use it to write more convincing phishing messages, imitate familiar styles of communication and search stolen information more quickly. It can also lower the barrier to entry, allowing less experienced attackers to work at greater speed and scale.

Businesses face a different challenge. Employees are often quick to experiment with new AI tools, sometimes before their organisation has decided what information can be shared or how the results should be checked. A member of staff might paste customer data, a contract or an internal report into a public platform without realising that confidential information has left the company’s controlled environment. This informal use is sometimes described as “shadow AI”.

CyPro helps organisations map where AI is already being used, identify the data involved and assign responsibility for each application. It can then support the creation of clear rules, including which tools are approved, which uses require permission and which activities are too risky. Keeping a central register also gives leaders a more accurate picture of how quickly AI is spreading through the organisation.
